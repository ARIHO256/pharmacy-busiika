# MyDoctor national health platform gap audit

## Scope and recommendation

This is a source audit of the React route tree, representative screens, Django models and the existing operations implementation report, benchmarked against published international examples. A proposed missing workspace means a dedicated route was not identified. It does not establish that every underlying operation is absent. Runtime usability, API permissions, clinical safety and national production integrations require separate validation.

The recommended first investment is a patient-centred worklist shared across reception, nursing, clinicians, pharmacy and diagnostics. Connect the existing visit, encounter, order, result and admission capabilities around a stable patient and facility context. Build identity, authorization, country configuration and exchange alongside that workflow so the first facilities exercise the foundations needed for national rollout.

## Design requirements across workspaces

- Keep patient identity, facility, encounter and care team visible while switching between tasks. Preserve the return path to the worklist and the active filters.
- Give each queue an owner, urgency, next action, due time and history. Design loading, empty, permission-denied, stale, offline, duplicate and conflicting-update states. Present corrections with provenance.
- Use task-specific workspaces for high-volume roles. In source, the shared Operations component handles multiple sections in one surface. Separate the busiest ward, referral, laboratory and identity workflows into dedicated screens that reuse its API responsibilities.
- Design patient and outreach views for small screens, keyboard and screen-reader navigation, low bandwidth and translated content. Test contrast, focus, form errors and accessible status updates against [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/).
- Replace hard-coded Uganda display assumptions with explicit country and facility configuration. Source examples include currency helpers in doctor/pharmacy/admin/patient, UGX invoice and price labels, and +256 registration hints. Use currency from the financial record, facility-local time with explicit time zone rules, configurable identifier and address formats, translations, selectable units and country-specific catalogues, payer rules and reporting schemas.

## International comparisons and where to apply them

- England’s national e-Referral Service links referrals with specialist booking. Use it as a benchmark for referring and receiving worklists and closed-loop scheduling. [NHS England](https://digital.nhs.uk/services/e-referral-service)
- Australia’s My Health Record includes clinical summaries and diagnostic reports. Its interoperability plan emphasizes identity, standards and information sharing. Apply those patterns to shared records, result provenance and national exchange onboarding. [Australian Department of Health](https://www.health.gov.au/topics/health-technologies-and-digital-health/my-health-record), [Australian Digital Health Agency](https://www.digitalhealth.gov.au/about-us/strategies-and-plans/national-healthcare-interoperability-plan)
- India’s Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission uses patient, facility and professional registries and consent-based exchange. Apply this pattern through country-specific registry adapters and visible consent scope, purpose and expiry. [Government of India](https://mospi.gov.in/uploads/publications_reports/publications_reports1783685273371_b09bf614-66a2-4320-9789-46c2cdfa0d63_Data_Harmonisation_Playbook-10Jul2026-compressed.pdf)
- Uganda publishes health information exchange and interoperability guidance. Use that guidance for the first country profile if Uganda is the pilot, and confirm the current specifications and onboarding process with the Ministry. [Uganda Ministry of Health](https://library.health.go.ug/index.php/file-download/download/public/1883)
- OpenHIE provides an architectural reference for separating registry, shared-record and exchange responsibilities. Use it to shape contracts with existing national services. [OpenHIE Architecture Specification](https://guides.ohie.org/arch-spec)
- WHO SMART Guidelines provide adaptation kits for antenatal care, immunization, HIV, TB, family planning and other programmes. Use them to define personas, workflows, data fields, decision logic and indicators with national programme owners. [WHO SMART Guidelines](https://www.who.int/teams/digital-health-and-innovation/smart-guidelines/)
- FHIR provides a basis for interoperability. The repo already has terminology adapters, a summary export and consent-gated partner delivery. Add conformance tests, acknowledgements, visible delivery failures, identifier mapping and approved profiles per jurisdiction. Confirm which exchanges use national APIs and which need local LIS/PACS or payer interfaces. [US ASTP/ONC](https://healthit.gov/interoperability/investments/fhir/)

## Delivery sequence and pilot acceptance

1. **P0, country and facility foundations.** Configure organization hierarchy, memberships, care relationships and access scope. Define consent and emergency access with local owners. Resolve identity without requiring a national ID at intake. Establish localization, notification policy, operational monitoring and tested restore procedures. Test cross-facility access and permitted sharing using independent organizations.
2. **P0, one complete care journey.** Pilot registration → booking or walk-in → triage → encounter → order → collection or dispense → reviewed result or medication follow-up → discharge or referral. Give every transition an owner. Verify that critical results are acknowledged or escalated, amended reports reach the right team, and retries leave a single authoritative action.
3. **P1, multi-site and national connections.** Add resource scheduling, external lab/imaging and payer connectivity, outreach, authority reporting and support worklists. Test outages, offline replay, failed exchanges, high-volume queues, migration, identity correction and restore during operational rehearsals.
4. **P2, specialty expansion.** Add specialty modules and cross-sector partners in the order agreed with launch countries and facilities. Track time to triage, referral closure, specimen rejection, critical-result acknowledgement, dispensing exceptions, reporting completeness and usability by role. Set targets from pilot baselines and clinical governance.
